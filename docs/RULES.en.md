# Mexican Train — Rules

> Edition 1.0 for the 0–6 set and two players. This is a translation. The
> Russian text in [RULES.ru.md](RULES.ru.md) is the primary version; if the
> two ever disagree, the Russian one is correct.
>
> Mexican Train is a traditional domino game. These rules adapt it to the
> ordinary 28-tile set and a two-player game.

## 1. The set and the deal

- 28 domino tiles from 0-0 to 6-6. Before every round the tiles are shuffled
  face down.
- Each player takes **7 tiles**. The remaining 14 form the **boneyard**: a
  player draws one tile from it when they cannot play.
- You see your own tiles; your opponent’s tiles and the boneyard are hidden.

## 2. The match and the engine

- A match is **7 rounds**. Each round has its own **engine** — the double the
  trains are built from: 6-6 in the first round, 5-5 in the second, and so on
  down to 0-0 in the seventh.
- The round’s double is part of the deal: it is in someone’s hand or in the
  boneyard.
- **A round opens with placing the engine.** The player who starts the round
  places the round’s double in the centre of the table. Without that double,
  they draw one tile from the boneyard: if it is the double, they place it;
  if not, the tile stays in their hand and the turn passes to the opponent.
  The opponent likewise places the double or draws — and so on in turn until
  the engine is placed.
- While the engine is being looked for, trains do not open (§3.4).
- The first regular move belongs to whoever placed the engine.

### Who starts a round

- The first round is decided by lot: each player draws a tile, and the one
  whose tile has the **lower** pip total starts.
- Every later round is started by the **winner of the previous round**
  (§5.3). If that round was tied, the round is started by the player who did
  not start the previous one.

## 3. Trains and a turn

1. Three chains of tiles — **trains** — run from the engine: each player’s
   personal train and the shared **Mexican train**. The first tile of any
   train joins the engine by the engine’s number; after that tiles follow by
   matching numbers, as in ordinary dominoes.
2. A turn is always **one tile** (except for a double, §4). It may be played:
   - on your own train;
   - on the Mexican train — it is always open to both players; either player
     may start it on their turn;
   - on your opponent’s train, if it is **open** (§3.4).
3. If you can play a tile, you must. Which tile and where is your choice.
4. If you cannot play, you draw **one** tile from the boneyard. If it fits,
   you must play that very tile. If it does not, it stays in your hand, the
   turn passes, and your personal train becomes **open**: your opponent may
   play on it. The train closes as soon as its owner plays on it.
5. If the boneyard is empty and you cannot play, you pass; your train opens.

## 4. Doubles

1. A double is placed crosswise on the train.
2. The player who placed a double **must cover it in the same turn** — join a
   tile with the same number to it. For example, 3-3 is placed on 4-3 — a
   tile with a three, say 3-5, must follow.
3. With nothing to cover it, the player draws one tile from the boneyard. If
   it fits, they play it on the double. If not, their train opens, the turn
   passes, and **the opponent must cover the double**: with a tile from their
   hand or one drawn from the boneyard. If they cannot either, their train
   opens too and the duty passes back.
4. **While a double is uncovered, no other tile may be played** — anywhere
   except on that double.
5. One tile is played per turn, or two with a double: the double and its
   continuation. Two doubles cannot be played in one turn.
6. **A double as the last tile** still has to be covered: the player with an
   empty hand draws a tile from the boneyard. If it covers the double, the
   round ends. If not, the tile stays in their hand, their train opens and
   the round goes on. If the boneyard is empty, the opponent must cover the
   double.

### A dead double

A double nothing can be joined to any more: all six other tiles bearing its
number are already on the table. Such a double is **dead** at once, the moment
it is played:

- it counts as an ordinary tile — it need not be covered, no tile is drawn
  for it, the player’s train does not open, and the turn passes as usual;
- **a train ending in a dead double is finished** — nobody plays on it any
  more;
- a dead double played as the last tile ends the round;
- on the table a dead double lies crosswise, face down.

## 5. End of a round and scoring

1. A round ends when:
   - a player has played their last tile and no double is uncovered — they
     **go out**;
   - the boneyard is empty and nobody can play — a **block**.
2. Each player scores the pip total of the tiles left in their hand. The
   player who went out scores 0. A **lone 0-0** — the only tile left in a
   hand — scores **25 points**; together with other tiles 0-0 scores 0.
3. The round is won by the player with the lower total in hand. If the totals
   are equal, the round has no winner.
4. After seven rounds the **lower total** for the match wins. Equal totals
   are a draw; the number of rounds won does not count.
